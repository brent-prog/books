The novel's central paradox

What happens when goodness meets the world?

The Idiot follows Prince Lev Myshkin, a man whose unusual honesty and compassion collide with a society shaped by pride, reputation, desire, jealousy and personal ambition. His goodness is genuine. Its consequences are not simple.

Dostoyevsky offers no neat management formula. Instead, he asks us to confront how little we know about other people—and how difficult it can be to act wisely even when our intentions are good.

The question beneath the book

Is goodness enough?

Compassion can be profound without being effective. Intelligence can be impressive without being kind. The novel holds both possibilities in tension.

That tension gives this work its relevance to leadership today.

A defining contradiction

The conflict between Heart & Mind

Perhaps the strongest bridge between Dostoyevsky's world and leadership is the challenge of combining humanity with judgment.
