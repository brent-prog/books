The Novel

Why The Idiot matters

A novel written more than 150 years ago can tell us a great deal about the people we deal with today. Times change. Technology changes. The world around us changes. Human nature doesn't change.

Through Prince Myshkin and the people around him, Dostoyevsky explores compassion, pride, vanity, insecurity, intelligence, freedom and the search for meaning. He also asks a difficult question: Is being a good person enough to make good decisions?

If we want to build better relationships, make better decisions and become better leaders, understanding people is a pretty good place to start.

A Closing Reflection

What The Idiot leaves us with

Dostoyevsky understood people.

He understood the contradiction between what we believe, what we want and how we actually behave. Compassion, pride, fear, ambition, honesty and insecurity all influence the decisions we make and how we treat each other.

The more we understand those motivations, the better equipped we are to make decisions, build relationships and lead others. People aren't simple, and understanding them takes work.

That is why The Idiot is worth reading, and worth returning to.
