02 · Think independently

Think from first principles. Think for yourself.

Thiel’s strongest pattern is not a tactic. It is a way of thinking: question formulas, challenge inherited assumptions and make a concrete plan for a future that has not been built yet.

The post-dot-com inversion

The conventional lessons Thiel says entrepreneurs absorbed, and the opposite principles he argues are more correct.

03 · Create and capture value

Escape competition.

Creating value is not enough. A valuable company also captures value. Thiel’s core strategic claim is that successful businesses become different enough to escape perfect competition, usually by solving a unique problem.

The path to durable monopoly

Start smallChoose a small, concentrated market with few or no competitors.
DominateWin a large share of that niche before expanding.
ExpandMove gradually into related and slightly broader markets.
EndureDesign for future cash flows and a defensible position decades out.
SOCIAL VALUE

Doing something different is what is truly good for society.

Thiel argues that creating genuinely new value does more for society than competing harder inside an existing category.

04 · Foundations

A startup messed up at its foundation cannot be fixed.

Thiel’s Law makes the founding conditions unusually important. The people, ownership structure, governance and incentives established early shape whether the company can stay aligned for the long term.

Ownership. Possession. Control.

Three different forms of power can exist inside the same company. Tap each to explore the distinction.

05 · Business evaluation

Seven questions every business must answer.

Thiel uses seven questions to test whether a business has the ingredients for a breakthrough, a defensible position and a viable path to customers. The cleantech failures in the book often missed one or more of them.

06 · Power law

We live under a power law.

A few exceptional outcomes matter far more than all ordinary outcomes combined.Companies, career choices, time and decisions can all have radically unequal importance.

07 · Go to market

If you build it, they will not just come.

Sales and distribution are not an afterthought. Thiel treats the ability to reach customers as part of the invention itself: one effective channel can make the business, while a kitchen-sink approach can leave every channel weak.

DISTRIBUTION ECONOMICSCustomer value must exceed acquisition cost.

A distribution channel only works economically when the value created by a customer exceeds what it costs to acquire that customer.

Work miracles. Empower people.

Technology creates the most value when it complements people rather than replaces them.Computers and humans are strongest when each does what the other cannot.
